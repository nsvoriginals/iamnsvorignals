OASIS

A virtual office you can actually walk through.

OASIS turns a video call into a place. It is a browser-based virtual office: a walkable floor, proximity voice and video that starts when you are close, room chat, screen sharing and whiteboards, and public or password-protected rooms. No account, no install — pick a name, choose an avatar and walk in.

Why a floor instead of a grid

Video calls flattened the office into a grid of faces. OASIS gives it floors, rooms and people again. A real floor plan means movement carries meaning: sitting at a desk says you are working, walking into the lounge says you are free to talk, and standing at a whiteboard says you are presenting. The interface follows the same idea — warm paper, hard ink borders, offset shadows, lime, coral and cobalt accents, and display type that scales with the room.

  • A real floor plan. Walk between a studio, meeting room, lounge and desk pod with a pixel avatar.
  • Proximity video and voice. Calls begin when you are close and end when you walk away.
  • Room chat. Messages, emoji and presence attached to the space you are in.
  • Screen sharing and whiteboards. Sit at a computer or stand at a board and present to the room.
  • Public and private rooms. Join the shared lobby or create a named room with an optional password.
  • Works on phones. A virtual joystick and responsive overlays replace the keyboard controls.
  • No account. Pick a name, choose an avatar and walk in. Nothing is stored after you leave.

Inside the build

The client is a Vite-built React app wrapped around a Phaser canvas. Phaser handles the floor, collisions, depth sorting and avatar movement through three scenes — Bootstrap, Game and Background. React renders everything around the canvas: the entry flow, room selection, room rail, chat, controls and marketing pages. Colyseus rooms keep players and messages in sync over WebSockets, while PeerJS establishes the WebRTC media streams for everyone standing near you. Redux Toolkit holds the session, room and presence state; the WebRTC and screen-share managers sit beside it as plain services.

  • World layer. Phaser 3 with Tiled maps, pixel characters, computers, whiteboards and vending machines. Pixel assets stay crisp with image-rendering: pixelated.
  • Network layer. Colyseus rooms for position, presence and chat; PeerJS for peer-to-peer media and screen sharing.
  • App layer. React Router routes for /, /play, /features, /about and /docs.
  • Responsive layer. Fluid clamp() display type, a 52px in-game rail on small screens, and overlays that stack above the joystick.

Controls

  • W A S D / arrow keys — walk around
  • E — sit down or stand up
  • R — share your screen at a computer
  • Enter — open chat
  • Esc — close chat or a dialog

Next environment pass

The world already ships with a Tiled floor map, office tilesets, pixel characters, computers, whiteboards, chairs and vending machines. The next pass makes it feel more alive without replacing the map: map variants for the lounge, studio and meeting room, layered ambient props, a slow day/night light shift, animated screens and vending machines, room tone and footsteps under the WebRTC audio, and detail tiers keyed to device width and frame budget.
